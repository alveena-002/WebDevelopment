# FitFlow — Design Direction

## Three directions considered

### Theme Name: Training Floor Utility
Very Brief Intro: A warm editorial SaaS system inspired by studio floor markings, membership cards, and high-energy noticeboards. It makes operational complexity feel tactile, clear, and human.
Probability: 0.07

### Theme Name: Night Shift Pulse
Very Brief Intro: A moody after-hours studio interface with cobalt ink, high-visibility lime, and athletic scoreboard cues. It feels energetic and premium without becoming a generic neon dashboard.
Probability: 0.04

### Theme Name: Soft Reset Club
Very Brief Intro: A calm, airy wellness direction using mineral neutrals, soft green, and quiet type. It is designed to feel restorative for yoga and recovery-led studios.
Probability: 0.02

## Chosen approach: Training Floor Utility

### Design Movement
Contemporary editorial modernism with Swiss grid discipline, retail wayfinding, and the physical language of a well-run training floor: taped zones, chalk marks, check-in boards, and clipped operational notes.

### Core Principles
1. **Operational clarity:** every card and label earns its place; feature content reads like a useful floor map, not a feature dump.
2. **Warm precision:** data is precise, but the surfaces feel physical through paper grain, rules, stamps, and restrained shadow.
3. **Asymmetric momentum:** compositions use offset panels, diagonal dividers, and editorial side notes instead of a centered SaaS stack.
4. **Proof over hype:** product claims are shown through believable UI states and specific UK studio workflows.

### Color Philosophy
The base is warm chalk paper rather than sterile white, giving the product a printed training-plan quality. Deep ink green provides calm authority and strong contrast. **Matcha Flash** is the ownable accent: a high-energy chartreuse that signals availability, progress, and action. Small touches of fired clay create human warmth for alerts and finance moments. The palette is bright and grounded, never “tech blue by default.”

### Layout Paradigm
Use a wide editorial canvas with a narrow left rail for context, a dominant product frame on the right, and offset sections that alternate between full-bleed paper and deep ink. The hero is a split composition: a compact statement column and a large product dashboard window with a floating “class availability” ticket. Avoid perfectly centered hero stacks.

### Signature Elements
1. **Floor-line rules:** thin chartreuse and ink rules that extend beyond cards like taped studio markings.
2. **Membership stamps:** small circular or rectangular labels such as `LIVE`, `UK READY`, and `WAITLIST OPEN`.
3. **Ticket-shaped UI notes:** availability, booking, and GMB moments appear as clipped operational slips rather than generic badges.

### Interaction Philosophy
Interactions should feel like moving a magnet on a studio board: quick, tactile, and explicit. Hover states nudge cards by a few pixels, buttons compress on press, and demo tabs swap with a short slide-and-fade. Every primary CTA should give immediate feedback, even when it is a front-end demo.

### Animation
Use 180–260ms ease-out transitions for controls. Entrance motion uses short upward translates with staggered delays on editorial blocks. Dashboard tabs slide 8px horizontally while fading. Avoid infinite ambient motion except for a very slow ticker and a subtle scanner line inside the QR check-in preview. Respect reduced-motion preferences.

### Typography System
Display: **Space Grotesk**, 600–700 for headlines and product numerals. Body: **DM Sans**, 400–600 for descriptions and navigation. Utility labels: **DM Mono**, 500, uppercase, with wide tracking. Headlines should be compact and slightly tight; supporting copy should stay between 60–72 characters per line.

### Brand Essence
FitFlow is the operating system for independent UK fitness spaces that want more full classes, fewer admin tabs, and a better member rhythm. Personality: **clear, spirited, dependable**.

### Brand Voice
Headlines are direct and active. CTAs sound like an invitation to see the system in motion, not a hard sell. Microcopy names the next useful action.

Example lines:
- “Run the floor. Grow the club.”
- “Turn every full class into a reason to come back.”

### Wordmark & Logo
The wordmark uses a custom geometric F with a cut-through motion bar, paired with a compact circular mark: a looped path entering a square corner, suggesting a member journey that always returns to the floor. The mark should be bold, text-free, and legible at favicon size.

### Signature Brand Color
**Matcha Flash — `#B8F45A`**. A bright, ownable chartreuse that reads as “space available” and “progress unlocked” against ink green and chalk paper.
